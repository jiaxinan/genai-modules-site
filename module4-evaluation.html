Module 4 of 6

Evaluating Responses as Texts

Apply rhetorical and writing concepts to AI output with a six-criterion checklist, then diagnose, revise, and retest.

⏱ ≈ 6 min in lectureCritiqueActivity

🎯Learning goals

  • Apply rhetorical concepts (audience, purpose, genre, stance, context) to AI output.
  • Use a six-criterion checklist to evaluate a response.
  • Diagnose problems, revise a prompt, and explain what changed.

📝AI responses are texts

Prompting and evaluation belong together. Before deciding how to revise a prompt, identify what is missing or ineffective in the response.

A response can be grammatically polished but poorly suited to its audience. It can answer part of a question while avoiding its central problem. It can sound balanced while leaving out a relevant perspective.

✅Evaluation checklist

CriterionQuestions to ask
Purpose and taskDoes the response answer the actual question and meet the instructions?
Audience and mediumAre its vocabulary, detail, and presentation appropriate?
Evidence and accuracyWhich claims can you verify? Do the cited sources exist and support them?
ReasoningDo the conclusions follow from the evidence? What assumptions remain unstated?
Perspective and contextWhose experiences appear? What relevant circumstances or viewpoints are missing?
UncertaintyDoes the response distinguish established information from speculation?
Point to specific passages. Replace “the response is biased” with an explanation of what it emphasizes or excludes, why that matters, and what evidence supports your judgment.

🛠️Activity: Diagnose, revise, and retest

Original prompt

Explain why misinformation spreads online and suggest a solution.

Hypothetical response (instructor-written, not an actual model result)

“Social media causes misinformation because users believe everything they see. The best solution is to ban anonymous accounts, which will eliminate false information.”

Step 1 · Diagnose

Identify two problems using the checklist.

Possible problems
  • An unsupported generalization about users (“believe everything they see”)
  • An oversimplified causal explanation (“social media causes…”)
  • An absolute claim about the proposed solution (“will eliminate”)

Step 2 · Revise

Write a revised prompt that addresses those problems.

One possible revision
Explain two mechanisms that can contribute to misinformation spreading online. Avoid assuming that all users behave alike. Evaluate one proposed intervention, including a limitation and a possible tradeoff. Use the supplied course reading to support your explanation. If the reading does not support a claim, identify that gap.

Step 3 · Retest

Run your revision and evaluate the new response with the same checklist. What would count as improvement?

A revision is a hypothesis about how to improve the answer. More instructions do not automatically produce better work.

📒For your AI critique assignment

Keep a record of:

  1. The original prompt
  2. The relevant output
  3. Your diagnosis
  4. The revised prompt
  5. What changed, including any new problems the revision introduced
