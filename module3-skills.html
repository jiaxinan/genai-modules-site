Module 3 of 6

Moving from Prompts to Skills

Turning a recurring prompt into a reusable skill (.md) file, and reading other people's skills critically.

⏱ ≈ 2 min in lecturePracticeTools

🎯Learning goals

  • Distinguish a one-off prompt from a reusable skill.
  • Describe what a critique skill might contain.
  • Read a published skill critically: its assumptions, its checks, and what it leaves to you.

📄Prompt vs. skill

Prompt

Instructions for a particular interaction. Written once, used once.

Skill

When a task recurs, its instructions are organized into a reusable document (often a .md file). This makes the procedure easier to reuse and revise.

A skill also carries our assumptions into future tasks, so we still need to check whether the procedure fits the new situation and whether its results are useful.

🧪Example: a critique skill outline

A critique skill might specify the materials to examine, the evaluation criteria, and the format of the feedback.

# AI Response Critique ## Materials to examine - The original prompt and the full AI response - The assigned course reading ## Evaluation criteria - Purpose and task, audience, evidence, reasoning, perspective, uncertainty (see Module 4) ## Feedback format - Quote the specific passage - Explain the problem and why it matters - Suggest a prompt revision to test

🔍Reading a skill critically

skills.sh is a directory that helps users discover reusable capabilities for compatible AI agents. Its listings and documentation let you examine what a skill does and how to use it.

Note: the leaderboard reflects installation activity, which does not establish educational quality or suitability for your assignment.

Begin by reading a skill's instructions. You can learn from its procedure even without installing it.

💬Think about it

Browse the directory and identify a task category you recognize. If you adopted one of those skills for coursework, which of its assumptions would you need to verify first?
