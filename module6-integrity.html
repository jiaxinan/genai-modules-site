Intellectual Contribution and Attribution

Separating your own analysis from AI assistance, and writing a disclosure that says what actually happened.

⏱ ≈ 3 min in lectureEthicsAttribution

🎯Learning goals

  • Distinguish intellectual contribution from attribution.
  • Explain why “I used AI” is not an adequate disclosure.
  • Write an accurate disclosure of your own process.

📖Key reading

In “Plagiarism, Copyright, and AI,” Mark A. Lemley and Lisa Larrimore Ouellette distinguish copyright questions from academic attribution and integrity. They argue that a general statement such as “I used AI” does not adequately explain which ideas or passages came from AI and which contributions came from the student. They also emphasize checking underlying sources.

🧭Two questions to keep separate

Intellectual contribution
What analysis, interpretation, or judgment did you contribute?
Attribution
Have you made clear where borrowed ideas, wording, and AI assistance entered the work?

Follow the course's rules about permitted AI use and disclosure. Adding your own analysis does not remove the need to credit others. Disclosing AI use does not automatically make every use permissible.

✍️Build your disclosure

An illustrative example. Adapt it only if it accurately describes your process.

I used [tool/model, date] to generate an initial explanation. I checked its claims against the assigned reading and identified an unsupported generalization. I revised the prompt to request evidence and limitations. The comparison and final evaluation are my own.
