Transformers and Language Models

Tokens, attention, and context: why the words around a word change its meaning, and why your prompt shapes the output.

⏱ ≈ 2.5 min in lectureConceptsVideo

🎯Learning goals

  • Explain tokens, numerical representations, and attention in plain language.
  • Use an example to show how context changes a word's meaning for a model.
  • Connect “one token at a time” generation to why prompts matter.

⚙️What is a transformer?

A transformer is a neural-network architecture used in many language models. Text enters the model as tokens, which can be words or parts of words. The model represents these tokens numerically. An attention mechanism allows it to combine information from different positions in the text, building representations that depend on context.

Attention is a mathematical operation, not attention in the human sense.

➡️Generating a response

In a typical text-generating LLM, the system produces a continuation one token at a time. Your prompt supplies part of the context that shapes that continuation. Changing the task description, examples, or source material can therefore change the response.

💬Think about it

If changing the context changes the output, what information should you include when asking for help with a course assignment?

Some possible answers

The assignment's actual question and instructions, the intended audience, the required length or format, the relevant course reading, and what you have already tried.
