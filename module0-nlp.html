Natural Language Processing: Next-Word Prediction

How language models began: predicting the next word from patterns in text, from n-gram counts to neural networks.

⏱ Pre-class in lectureConceptsWarm-up

🎯Learning goals

  • Describe a language model as a system that predicts the next word (token) from context.
  • Contrast a statistical n-gram model with a neural approach such as an RNN.
  • Recognize that predictions reflect the data a model was trained on.

🧩Try it: fill in the blank

Before reading further, make your own prediction.

“I am going to reply to the ____”

An illustrative probability distribution a model might assign based on its dataset

email46%
message22%
comment14%
question11%
letter7%

These numbers are invented for teaching. A real model scores every token in its vocabulary; the top choice is often, but not always, the one it outputs. Each choice shows a different hypothetical dataset in which that word most often follows “reply to the.”

📚Two ways to predict “email”

Statistical n-gram model

Counts how often “email” follows “reply to the” in a training corpus (for example, one available through NLTK), then picks the most frequent continuation. Simple and transparent, but it only sees a few words back.

Recurrent neural network (RNN)

Learns patterns from training on sequential data, such as messaging datasets. It carries a running representation of earlier words, so it can use longer context than a fixed n-gram window.

Modern large language models keep the same basic goal, predicting what comes next, but use a different architecture: the transformer.

💬Think about it

If a model's prediction depends on how often words appeared together in its training data, whose language and whose situations are most likely to be represented well? Which might be missing?
